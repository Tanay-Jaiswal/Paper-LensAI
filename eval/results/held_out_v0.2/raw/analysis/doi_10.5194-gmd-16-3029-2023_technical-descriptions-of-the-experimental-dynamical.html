## Summary

- To understand sources of uncertainties in global variable-resolution (VR) dynamical downscaling, we produced a regional climate dataset using the Model for Prediction Across Scales (MPAS; dynamical core version 4.0) coupled to the Community Atmosphere Model (CAM; version 5.4), which we refer to as CAM–MPAS hereafter. *(p.1)*
- K. Sakaguchi et al.: Technical descriptions of the experimental dynamical downscaling simulations Figure 13. *(p.24)*

**Problem**
- However, limited by computing resources and model structures, climate projections produced by global climate and Earth system models, including those in the most recent Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 6 (CMIP6; Eyring et al., 2016), are mostly available at grid spacing of 100–150 km. *(p.2)*
- Few studies have compared limited-area and global VR dynamical downscaling approaches at the climate timescale (Hagos et al., 2013; Huang et al., 2016; Xu et al., 2018, 2021), making such comparisons an important element of the HyperFACETS project. *(p.3)*

**Results**
- However, few attempts (e.g., Wilby et al., 2000) have been made to compare different statistical and dynamical downscaling methods under the same experimental protocol to reduce the factors confounding interpretation of the results. *(p.2)*
- Dynamical downscaling usually refers to numerical simulations over a limited-area domain to achieve a higher resolution than those of global climate models (e.g., Giorgi and Mearns, 1991; Giorgi, 2019). *(p.2)*
- Having both limited-area and VR models in a coordinated project may also facilitate interactions between global and regional climate modeling communities, which could accelerate model development and workflow improvement to further reduce uncertainties in the regional climate dataset. *(p.25)*
